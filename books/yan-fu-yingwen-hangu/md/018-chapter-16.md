# 篇十六 造句集例 SUMMARY OF THE RULES OF SYNTAX

§ 150. RULE 1

A Verb Must Agree with Its Nominative (Subject) in Number and Person: 云謂之字，必與其主名之稱身奇偶相得；如云“The *sun shines*,” but “The *stars shine*.”

Rule 1, A. Every Verb, Except an Infinitive, Must Have a Subject: 凡云謂之字，除無候式外，必有其主名。

Rule 1, B. Every Subject Must Have a Verb, Except: 凡為主名，必有其云謂，除二事：

（子）主名獨用（Nominative absolute）：如云“*Napoleon having been defeated*, the allies entered Paris.”

（丑）主名呼用（Nominative of address）：如云“*Gentlemen*, be seated.”

Rule 1, C. It is Improper to Use Both a Noun and Its Pronoun as a Subject of the Same Verb: 凡同一云謂之字，不得以名物稱代二字並施為主名；如云“*The general he* rode away,”乃窳語也，去 *he* 字乃是。

Rule 1, D. Two or More Singular Subjects, Connected by *and*, Take a Plural Verb: 兩單名以上，用 *and* 挈合為主名者，其云謂必作複言；如云“*Gold and iron are* metals.”

Rule 1, E. When Singular Subjects, Connected by *and*, Express One Idea, They Take a Singular Verb: 兩單名以 *and* 挈合，若共表一事一物者，其云謂用單言；如云“*Bread and butter is* good for breakfast.”“*A block and tackle was* used for hoisting it.”“*The horse and chaise is* at the door.”

Rule 1, F. Singular Subjects, Connected by *and*, Preceded by *each*, *every*, *no*, Take a Singular Verb: 兩單名以 *and* 挈合，若其前有 *each*, *every*, *no* 等區別字者，其云謂亦用單言；如云“*Every officer and every soldier was* at his post.”“*No thought, no word, no act of mine, was* known to him.”

Rule 1, G. Singular Subjects, Connected by *as well as*, Take a Singular Verb: 兩單名以 *as well as* 挈合者，其云謂用單言；如云“*Caesar, as well as Cicero, was* an orator.”

Rule 1, H. When the Subjects are of Different Persons or Numbers, the Verb Agrees with the First: 兩名稱身奇偶不同，以 *as well as* 挈合者，其云謂字，必與前名相得；如云“*He, as well as they, was* pleased.”“*They, as well as he, were* pleased.”“*I, as well as you, am* mistaken.”

§ 151. RULE 2

Singular Subjects, Connected by *or* or *nor*, Take a Singular Verb: 凡單名以 *or* 或 *nor* 挈合者，其云謂必用單言；如云“*Either John or his brother was* there.”“*Neither gold nor iron was* to be seen.”

Rule 2, A. When Two Subjects are of Different Persons, the Verb Agrees with the Nearer: 兩主名以 *or* 或 *nor* 挈合，而稱身不同者，其云謂必與近己之主名相得；如云“*Either he or I am* wrong.”“*Neither you nor he is* in fault.”

Rule 2, B. It is Better, However, to Repeat the Verb for Each Subject: 上言各例，語多迫隘，若欲得體，不如重舉其云謂；如云“*Either he is* wrong, *or I am*.”“*Neither are you* in fault, *nor is he*.”

Rule 2, C. When Subjects are of Different Numbers, the Verb Agrees with the Nearer: 兩主名奇偶不同，其云謂與近己之名相得；如云“*Neither the general nor the officers were* present.”“*Neither the officers nor the general was* present.”

Rule 2, D. The Plural Subject, However, is Better Placed Near the Verb: 主名奇偶不同，複者在後為勝；故上二例，以後例為不如前例也。

Rule 2, E. When Subjects are of Different Numbers or Persons, It is Often Best to Repeat the Verb: 兩名奇偶稱身不同者，亦以重舉云謂為勝；如云“*Neither was the general* present, *nor were the officers*.”

Rule 2, F. When Singular Subjects are Accompanied by *with*, *together with*, *in addition to*, the Verb is Singular: 單名之後，附以 *with*, *together with*, *in addition to* 者，其云謂用單言；如云“*The ship, with* all its crew, *was* lost.”“*The carriage, together with* the horses, *was* damaged.”“*The horse, in addition to* the harness, *was* sold.”

§ 152. RULE 3

A Collective Noun, Denoting the Collection as a Whole, Takes a Singular Verb; When It Denotes the Individuals, It Takes a Plural Verb: 聚眾之名，若以全體為言，其云謂用單言；若指眾中之分子，其云謂用複言；如云“*The parliament was* dissolved.”此以全體言也；“*The committee were* not unanimous.”此舉分子言也。

Rule 3, A. The Collection as a Whole: 全體之例：“*The assembly was* large.”“*The fleet has* sailed.”

Rule 3, B. The Individuals: 分子之例：“*The people were* divided in their opinions.”“*The jury have* failed to agree.”

Rule 3, C. The Verb and the Pronoun Must Correspond in Number: 名指全體，則云謂及稱代俱用單；名指分子，則云謂及稱代俱用複；不得此單而彼複，此複而彼單；如云“*The committee has* finished *its* work.”不得作“*The committee has* finished *their* work.”或“*The committee have* finished *its* work.”也。亦不得云“*The army has* completed the campaign, but *they* have lost many men.”而宜云“*The army has* completed the campaign, but *the men* have suffered heavily.”“The party *is* broken up, and *its* members have joined the opposition,” not “*their* members have joined.”

§ 153. RULE 4

Adjectives Qualify Nouns and Pronouns: 區別字所以限釋名物稱代之字；如云“A *wise* man.”“*He* is *wise*.”

Rule 4, A. *This* and *that* Agree with Their Nouns in Number: 指名之字，若 *this* 若 *that*，必與其所指之名奇偶相得；如云“*this* sort of book,” but “*these* sorts of books.”“*that* kind of tree,” but “*those* kinds of trees.”

§ 154. RULE 5

The Distributive Adjectives—*each*, *every*, *either*, *neither*—Qualify Singular Nouns: 區物之字，如 *each*, *every*, *either*, *neither*，必用於單名；如云“*Each* boy had *his* book,” not “*their* book.”“*Every* man must do *his* duty,” not “*their* duty.”“*Neither* of them *was* satisfied,” not “*were* satisfied.”

Rule 5, A. *Either* and *neither* Refer to One of Two Objects; *any*, *none*, to One of More than Two: *Either*, *neither* 二字，用於兩物；若物在三以上，則用 *any* 或 *none*；如云“*Either* of the two books,” but “*Any* of the three books.”“*Neither* of the two men,” but “*None* of the three men.”

Rule 5, B. The Comparative Degree is Used in Comparing Two Objects; the Superlative, in Comparing More than Two: 兩物相較，區別字用次級；三物以上相較，用極級；如云“He is the *taller* of the two.”“He is the *tallest* of the three.”

Rule 5, C. When Comparing Objects of the Same Class, the Comparative Requires the Use of *other*; the Superlative Excludes It: 次級相比，於同類之物，必用 *other* 字；極級則反是，不得用 *other* 字；如云“London is *larger than any other city* in England,” not “*larger than any city* in England;” because London is a city in England, and cannot be larger than itself. But we say: “London is the *largest of all cities* in England,” not “the *largest of all other cities* in England;” because London is included in the number.

§ 155. RULE 6

Pronouns Agree in Gender, Number, and Person with the Nouns for Which They Stand: 稱代之字，必與其所代之名陰陽、奇偶、稱身相合；如云“The *boy* loves *his* mother.”“The *girl* loves *her* father.”“The *trees* shed *their* leaves.”

Rule 6, A. Pronouns Referring to Two or More Singular Nouns Connected by *and* Must be Plural: 稱代二名以上，以 *and* 挈合者，稱代字必用複；如云“*John and his brother* have lost *their* way.”

Rule 6, B. Pronouns Referring to Two or More Singular Nouns Connected by *or* or *nor* Must be Singular: 稱代二名以上，以 *or* 或 *nor* 挈合者，稱代字用單；如云“*Either John or his brother* will lend *his* aid.”“*Neither the general nor the officer* could find *his* horse.”

Rule 6, C. When Pronouns Refer to Nouns of Different Genders Connected by *or* or *nor*, It is Better to Use a Pronoun for Each: 若名之陰陽不同，以 *or*, *nor* 挈合者，稱代字宜各舉其類；如云“*Neither John nor his sister* has received *his or her* wages.”

Rule 6, D. When the Sex is Not Known, the Masculine Pronoun is Generally Used: 若性之陰陽不可知，則從陽類；如云“*Every person* must judge for *himself*.”

Rule 6, E. Pronouns Referring to *each*, *every*, *everyone*, *everybody*, *anybody*, *nobody*, Must be Singular: 名有 *each*, *every*, *everyone*, *everybody*, *anybody*, *nobody* 等字者，其稱代字必用單；如云“*Each* of the pupils has *his* task.”“*Everyone* must do *his* duty,” not “*their* duty.”“*Nobody* knows what *he* can do until *he* tries.”

Rule 6, F. *It* is Often Used Indefinitely: *It* 字常泛指無定；如云“*It* rains.”“*It* is cold.”“*It* is true that he said so.”

Rule 6, G. *It* is Also Used to Introduce a Sentence, Whatever the Number or Person of the Following Subject: *It* 字又常冠首起句，其後名詞，無論稱身奇偶，皆可相從；如云“*It* is *I*.”“*It* was *they*.”“*It* was *the boys* that made the noise.”

Rule 6, H. The Relative Pronoun Must Agree with Its Antecedent in Gender, Number, and Person: 複牒稱代之字，必與其所牒之名，陰陽、奇偶、稱身相得；如云“The *man who* came.”“The *woman who* came.”“The *book which* was lost.”“The *books which* were lost.”

Rule 6, I. The Case of a Relative Pronoun Depends on Its Construction in Its Own Clause: 複牒稱代字之位，視其在本小句中之句法而定；如云“The man *whom* I saw.”（受事位）“The man *whose* hat was blown off.”（領有位）“The man *who* spoke to me.”（主名位）

Rule 6, J. If as is Used as a Relative, the Following Verb Must Agree with the Noun Represented: 假 *as* 字作複牒稱代用，其下之云謂字，必與其所牒之名相得；如云“His statements are as *follow*,” but “His statement is as *follows*.”[^p243_1]

Rule 6, K. If No Nominative Comes between a Relative and a Verb, the Relative Must be Put in the Nominative Case: 若複牒稱代與云謂字之間，無他主名，則複牒稱代字為主名位；如云“The man *who was* sick.”若有他主名，則為受事位，或介字之受事位；如云“The man *whom I saw*.”“The man *to whom I spoke*.”[^p243_2]

§ 156. RULE 7

Transitive Verbs and Their Participles Govern the Objective Case: 及物之云謂，與其兩用之式，皆役受事之位；如云“I *love him*.”“*Seeing them*, I was glad.”

Rule 7, A. The Objective Case Usually Follows the Verb: 受事位常在云謂字之後；如云“Brutus *killed Caesar*.”

Rule 7, B. But When It is a Relative or Interrogative Pronoun, It Precedes the Verb: 複牒及詢問稱代字，常在云謂字之前；如云“The man *whom I met*.”“*Whom* did you see?”

Rule 7, C. Intransitive Verbs Take No Objective Case: 不及物云謂，不役受事位；如云“He *sleeps*.”“The bird *flies*.”

Rule 7, D. But Intransitive Verbs May Take a Cognate Objective: 但不及物云謂，可役同氣之受事位；如云“He *ran a race*.”“She *slept a quiet sleep*.”“He *died a glorious death*.”

Rule 7, E. Some Intransitive Verbs, by the Addition of a Preposition, Become Transitive: 不及物云謂，綴以介系之字，遂成及物；如云“To *laugh at* a man.”“To *wonder at* his folly.”“To *prevail over* an enemy.”

Rule 7, F. Verbs of Teaching, Asking, Giving, etc., Take Two Objectives, One Direct and the Other Indirect: 敎受、詢問、畀與等云謂字，常役二受事位，一為直接，一為間接；如云“He taught *me* (indirect) *English* (direct).”“He asked *me* (indirect) *a question* (direct).”

Rule 7, G. When Such Verbs are Turned into the Passive Voice, Either Objective May be Made the Subject: 此等句法改為受動式者，或以此受事為主名，或以彼受事為主名；如云“*I* was taught English by him,” or “*English* was taught me by him.”

Rule 7, H. The Passive Voice of Transitive Verbs is Followed by a Preposition: 及物云謂改為受動式者，其後常綴以介系之字；如云“He was killed *by* a fall.”“The letter was written *with* a pen.”

§ 157. RULE 8

The Verb *to be* and Some Other Intransitive Verbs Take the Same Case After Them as Before Them: *To be* 及他不及物云謂，前後之位必同；如云“*It* is *I*,” not “*It* is *me*.”“*It* was *he*,” not “*It* was *him*.”“I knew *it* to be *him*,” not “to be *he*.”

Rule 8, A. Other Verbs of This Kind are: *become*, *seem*, *appear*, *stand*, *walk*, *live*, *die*, etc.: 他不及物字如 *become*, *seem*, *appear*, *stand*, *walk*, *live*, *die* 等，亦同此例；如云“He *became a soldier*.”“He *seems an honest man*.”“He *died a martyr*.”

Rule 8, B. Passive Verbs of Calling, Naming, Appointing, Making, etc., Take the Same Case After Them as Before Them: 召號、定名、補授、使為等云謂，用受動式者，前後之位必同；如云“He was called *John*.”“He was made *king*.”“He was appointed *governor*.”

Rule 8, C. Active Verbs of the Same Class Take Two Objectives: 上項云謂字，用主動式者，則役二受事位；如云“They called him *John*.”“They made him *king*.”“They appointed him *governor*.”

Rule 8, D. The Nominative Following the Verb is Called the Predicate Nominative: 隨云謂字之後為主名位者，名曰標表主名位（Predicate nominative）；如云“He is a *doctor*.”

Rule 8, E. The Objective Following the Verb is Called the Predicate Objective: 隨云謂字之後為受事位者，名曰標表受事位（Predicate objective）；如云“They made him *captain*.”

§ 158. RULE 9

The Possessive Case is Governed by the Noun Which Follows It: 領有之位，役於其後之名物字；如云“*John’s* book.”“*Man’s* life.”

Rule 9, A. The Governing Noun is Sometimes Omitted: 役主之名，有時省去；如云“I went to *St. Paul’s* (church).”

Rule 9, B. When Two or More Nouns Possess Something in Common, the Sign of the Possessive is Put After the Last Only: 二名以上同領一物者，領號（’s）只施於末一名；如云“*John and James’s* father.”“*Messrs. Smith and Son’s* office.”

Rule 9, C. When Two or More Nouns Possess Things Separately, the Sign of the Possessive is Put After Each: 二名以上各領一物者，則一一皆施領號；如云“*John’s and James’s* books.”“*Smith’s and Brown’s* stores.”

Rule 9, D. When a Noun in the Possessive is Accompanied by Words Explanatory of It, the Sign is Put After the Explanatory Words: 名在領有位，而有別字以申明之者，領號施於申明字之後；如云“The *King of England’s* crown.”“At *Smith the bookseller’s* shop.”

Rule 9, E. A Noun Governing the Possessive Case is Not Limited to One Word: 役領有位之名，不必限於一字；如云“*The captain’s and crew’s* efforts saved the ship.”

Rule 9, F. The Possessive Sign is Sometimes Attached to a Clause: 領號有施於一句者；如云“The *Lord Mayor of London’s* banquet.”

§ 159. RULE 10

One Verb Governs Another in the Infinitive Mood: 一云謂役他云謂於無候式；如云“I *desire to learn*.”“He *wishes to go*.”

Rule 10, A. The Infinitive is Generally Preceded by the Preposition *to*: 無候式常冠以 *to* 字；如云“*To err* is human; *to forgive*, divine.”

Rule 10, B. The Infinitive is Sometimes Used Without *to* After the Verbs: *bid*, *dare*, *feel*, *hear*, *make*, *need*, *see*, and Some Others: 在 *bid*, *dare*, *feel*, *hear*, *make*, *need*, *see* 等云謂字之後，無候式不冠 *to* 字；如云“I bade him *go*.”“I heard him *sing*.”“I saw him *fall*.”

Rule 10, C. The Infinitive is Often Used as a Noun: 無候式常作名物字用；如云“*To read* is useful.”“I like *to read*.”

Rule 10, D. The Infinitive May be Used Adjectively or Adverbially: 無候式可作區別字或疏狀字用；如云“Water *to drink*.”（區別）“He came *to see* me.”（疏狀）

Rule 10, E. The Infinitive is Sometimes Used Absolutely: 無候式有時獨立自為句；如云“*To tell the truth*, I do not believe it.”

§ 160. RULE 11

Adverbs Qualify Verbs, Adjectives, and Other Adverbs: 疏狀之字，限釋云謂、區別，及他疏狀之字；如云“He speaks *fluently*.”“A *very* good book.”“He speaks *very fluently*.”

Rule 11, A. Adverbs are Generally Placed Before the Words They Qualify, but After Verbs: 疏狀字常居其所限釋字之前，但在云謂字之後；如云“A *greatly* improved edition.”“He *read slowly*.”

§ 161. RULE 12

Two Negatives in English Destroy Each Other, and are Equivalent to an Affirmative: 英文以二不相乘，遂成印可；如云“I can*not* do *no* more,”意乃謂“我能多為”也；若欲表“不能多為”之意，則宜云“I cannot do *any* more.”“His language, though simple, is *not in*elegant”意乃謂“其文頗雅”也。

§ 162. RULE 13

Prepositions Govern the Objective Case: 介系之字，皆役受事位；如云“He went *to town*.”“The book is *on the table*.”“He came *with me*.”

§ 163. RULE 14

Conjunctions Connect Words and Sentences: 挈合之字，連綴名詞及句；如云“John *and* James.”“He went, *but* I remained.”

Rule 14, A. Conjunctions Connect the Same Cases of Nouns and Pronouns, and the Same Moods and Tenses of Verbs: 挈合之字，其所連綴之名物稱代，其位必同，其所連綴之云謂字，其式其候必同；如云“I saw *him and her*,” not “*him and she*.”“He *spoke and wrote* well,” not “*spoke and writes*.”

§ 164. RULE 15

Interjections Have No Grammatical Connection with Other Words: 嗟歎之字，與他字無句法上之關係；如云“*Alas!* I am undone.”“*O*, virtue, how amiable thou art!”

§ 165. RULE 16

The Subjunctive Mood is Used to Express a Condition, Supposition, Doubt, Wish, etc.: 偏倚之式，用以表條約、擬想、疑惑、願欲諸事；如云“*If he were* here, he would help us.”“*Would that he were* alive!”

Rule 16, A. The Determination of the Speaker is Expressed by *will* in the First Person, and *shall* in the Second and Third: 於第一身用欲，於第二三身用須者，以見言者之有所主張，不僅說將來之事而已；如云“I *will* not give in,”“Thou *shalt* not lie.”

§ 166. RULE 17

The Participle is Often Used Absolutely: 兩用式之云謂常可獨用；如云“*Calculating roughly*, it will cost five pounds.”

Rule 17, A. When a Participle is Used as an Adjective, the Active is to Qualify a Noun Which is an Agent of the Action Signified by the Original Verb; and the Passive, to Qualify an Object of the Action: 以兩用式之云謂為區別字用者，須分施受，施者著其物之所能為，受者著其物之所受事；如云“A *galloping* horse,”方馳之馬，馬能馳也；“The *galloped* horse,”所馳之馬，馬之被驅者也。假如以繩量物，量物之繩稱“The *measuring* line,”若云“The *measured* line,”則此繩為他度所量者矣。“The *loving* son,”孝子也，愛親之子也；“The *loved* son,” or “the *beloved* son,”驕兒也，為親所愛者也。中國初學人，往往於二者不能辨，於句法之剛聲柔聲亦然。

§ 167. RULE 18

Certain Words Must be Followed by Particular Prepositions: 用介系字於虛實各字之下，常有定法；如云“I *confide* (倚信) *in* your promise,”於 *confide* 之下必用 *in* 字為介系，設用他字如 *to*, *on*, *through* 等字，便不可通。他若 *similar* 之後用 *to*, *different* 之後用 *from*, 而 *difference* 之後又用 *between*, 皆定例也；此當於讀文字聽言語時留意，久則自知，今欲求便學人，為列百餘常用之字如下，不能盡也。

Rule 18. A. To or *for* is Generally Used Before Places After Verbs of Motion, *In* or *at* is Used After Verbs of Rest: 云謂字之言動趨者，其下有名物以指其地，所用介系多 to 與 for；如云“He goes *to* Shanghai,”“The ship sails *for* Foochow”是已。云謂之言居處者，於地名之前，多用 in 與 at；如云“He lived *in* Peiping,”“The cargo is landed *at* Tangku”是已。But in is generally used before countries, districts, etc., and at before a station or any other comparatively small places: 然 in 之正譯為在，at 之正譯為當，故 in 字常用於郡，省，國，島，之前，at 字則加於一鎮一站之上，蓋在者見其所容，當者著其定點，苟知此義，自不至或誤用也。

§ 168. RULE 19

Some Conjunctions go in Pairs Called Correlatives; Used as *either*—*or*; *whether*—*or*; *neither*—*nor*; *though*—*yet*; *both*—*and*; *as*—*as*: 挈合字，有相應為用者。

Rule 19, A. The Conjunction *that* is Usually Omitted: 挈合字 *that*，常以介紹子句，然可勿用，以著簡潔；如云“He knows *I must go*,”後三字乃受事子句，法當用 *that* 字引起，然不用，語亦通也。

§ 169. RULE 20

In Colloquial Language Objective Case is Sometimes Used in the Place of Nominative: 談言之際，有宜用主名，而當以受事之位者；如云“That’s *him*”＝that is he; “Who is there? *Me*, sir,” “*Whom* do men say that I am,”皆此類也，而不得以破例論。

Rule 20, A. The Objective Case is Used in Exclamations: 歎叱之詞，常用第一身之受事位；如云“Dear *me*!”“Ah *me*!”“Oh *me* unhappy!”“*Me* to be thus jeered at!”

§ 170. RULE 21

Great Caution Must be Used in Elliptical Sentences (especially with *as* and *than*) to see the Right Cases are Used. The Best Way is to Fill Up the Ellipsis: 橢句審位常難，須先將所橢削者補足，則其義自見；如云“He loves me better than [*he loves*] *thee*,”此見其所比擬者在受事；又如“He loveth me better than *thou* [*lovest me*]”此見其所比擬者在主名也。
